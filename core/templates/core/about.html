About OSPREY

OSPREY — Open Science Platform for Research and Engineering. A public-benefit commons for open research engineering.

The problem

Two graduate students in different labs are designing the same underwater peristaltic pump right now, and neither knows the other exists. Both designs will mostly work. Both will end up flattened into one sentence in a paper or thesis: “a custom peristaltic pump was used.” Next year, a third student starts from zero. Either of those designs could have saved weeks of work, but neither is findable. This pattern holds across experimental fields, and it's a quiet drag on how fast science moves.

Good designs already exist out there, and many researchers and engineers are willing to share them if they're asked. But formally open-sourcing a design is a lot of effort with often no benefit to the creator. How would somebody find their design even if they did open source it? And if nobody can find it, why bother documenting and open-sourcing it in the first place?

What OSPREY is

OSPREY is a proposed commons for open-sourced research engineering. If you've used Printables or Thingiverse for 3D-printable models, the idea is similar: a place where people upload designs with enough metadata that other researchers can actually find them. The main difference is that OSPREY is built around how academic research credit works.

When you publish a project, it gets a citable DOI. If somebody uses your design, they're expected to cite it in any resulting publication or, when a citation isn't a natural fit, leave a user attestation on the project page. OSPREY tracks both. Over time a project accumulates a public record of who used it and what came out of that use.

OSPREY also gives projects the tools to make their open-source efforts succeed: community wikis for documentation, issue tracking so people can report problems and suggest improvements, and basic governance structures so a project can grow beyond one person maintaining it alone.

When somebody forks a project to adapt it for their own work, OSPREY records a lineage edge from the new version back to the original. Credit propagates through the tree, so the engineer who did the foundational work still gets recognized when somebody three forks down publishes a paper using a descendant of their pump. And at any time, somebody can look at the lineage tree to see the path a project has taken to get where it is, and to find related projects worth knowing about.

The point of all of this is to make open-sourcing engineering work easier, and to make it feel worth doing. People who upload, contribute to, and maintain designs that get used build a visible record of that contribution, in a form that hiring committees and funders can actually evaluate. And researchers looking for a design can search a catalog of projects that have been used and tested in real labs.

The future I want to build

In ten years, when a new graduate student is asked to build a pump, their first move is to search OSPREY's commons of research engineering. They find three mature, well-documented designs. They pick the closest fit, adapt it, and have a working prototype in a week. The original authors get credit every time someone uses the design. The student can submit improvements back, and the next person who comes along starts from their version.

Every research field would have a handful of canonical reference designs that the community has converged on, maintains together, and improves over time. Researchers get credit for useful engineering work, tracked through real reuse. Funders and hiring committees treat open source engineering contributions as real, measurable outputs in evaluations. And open-sourcing a design becomes a standard publishing step because it just makes sense.

Where the project is at

Right now I'm building a demo. A small web app that shows the core platform working with a couple of reference designs. Alongside that, I'm starting conversations with a few labs and a couple of peer institutions about what they'd want from a tool like this, and looking at potential grant routes for funding the next phase.

OSPREY is run by Jonathan A Pfeifer through pHalkon Industries LLC as a public-benefit project. If you'd like to help build it, or use the demo with your own work, get in touch.
